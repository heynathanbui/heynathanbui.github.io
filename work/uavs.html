01 · The brief

A student association runs on turnout.

UAVS NSW brings together Vietnamese students across New South Wales. It runs on volunteers, sponsors and people showing up, so every event has to do three jobs at once: get students through the door, give sponsors visible value, and leave content that keeps the community active after the day.

Media and design is where those three jobs meet. The identity sells the event before it happens; the photos are what people keep, tag and share once it’s over.

Every team photo carries the event name, UAVS and the sponsors.
Job 01 · Attention

Give each event its own world

Students scroll past generic posters. A distinct concept per event (an 8-bit game, a heritage-meets-tech hackathon) makes each one recognisable at a glance.

Job 02 · Sponsors

Build sponsors into the frame

Sponsor tiers sit on every key visual and photo backdrop, so each shared photo is also sponsor exposure, without a separate ad.

Job 03 · Memory

Photos are the product

After the event, the album is what remains. Portraits people want to post and crowd shots people tag themselves in keep the event travelling.

03 · Strategy

An event is a funnel.

A student event competes with every other thing a student could do that weekend. I treat each one as a five-stage funnel, and give every stage its own asset and its own number. That turns “make a poster” into a plan: if registrations are low, the problem is at stage two, not stage four.

Why identity matters commercially

The sponsor value loop

UAVS events are funded by sponsors, so media is not decoration, it is the product sponsors are buying. Each identity is built so the sponsor lockup appears in the key visual, on the backdrop and on the prizes. Every photo a participant shares then delivers a sponsor impression at no extra cost, which is the evidence the partnerships team needs at renewal.

Step 1

Design it in

What
Sponsor tiers placed on poster, cover, backdrop and prize boards from the first draft.

Step 2

Make it shareable

What
Portraits and winner photos people actually want to post, with the lockup in frame.

Step 3

Report it back

What
Reach and shares of sponsor-carrying posts, packaged per sponsor after each event.

04 · Measurement plan

What I’d track, and with what.

A volunteer-run association rarely has a measurement setup. This is the lightweight stack I would put in place: free tools, one source of truth per stage, and a single sheet that compares events so each one teaches the next.
