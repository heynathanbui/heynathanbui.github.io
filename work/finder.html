01 · The brief

Finance is useful. It’s rarely fun.

Finder helps Australians compare credit cards, loans, banking, insurance, investing and utilities. It started in Sydney in 2006 and now runs in the UK, US and Canada too.

Money and crypto content is dense and easy to scroll past. My job across four workstreams was to make it entertaining enough to stop for, and easy enough to find.

Finance also has a trust problem. Google treats money topics as “Your Money or Your Life” content and puts extra weight on experience, expertise and trustworthiness. So the work had to entertain without ever undermining credibility.

Constraint 01 · Attention

Low-interest category

Few people want to think about finance on social. Content has to earn the stop in the first seconds, then deliver something useful.

Constraint 02 · Trust

Credibility is the product

A comparison site lives on being trusted. Humour, founder content and video all have to reinforce that, not trade it away for reach.

Constraint 03 · Discovery

Social reach vs search demand

Social feeds reward entertainment; search rewards answers. Covering both means different formats for different intent.

Tab03

Founder-led content · team project

Fred Schebesta’s personal brand

People follow people, not comparison tables.

With a team of fellow interns, I worked on personal-brand content for Finder co-founder Fred Schebesta. Founder-led content gives a company a human face. People trust people more than logos, and a founder’s point of view travels further than a brand post, especially in a category where trust decides who you buy from.

The example here is an episode I co-wrote and edited, filmed by a teammate: Top 200 Coins, a series on the Crypto Finder channel where Fred analyses one token per episode. A numbered series does three jobs at once: it gives the founder a repeatable format instead of one-off opinions, each episode targets a search for a specific token, and a ten-minute analysis can be cut into Shorts that send viewers back to the full video.

Reading the feed

A series builds the habit. The one-offs bring in new people.

The daily update is the backbone: a template that can be published every day without a new idea each time, which keeps the channel active and gives returning viewers a reason to come back. The how-to, founder and event Shorts sit on top of it. They are the ones with a chance of reaching someone new, through search, through Fred, or through a share. A Short is also the cheapest test available: a hook that holds attention for 20 seconds is evidence the topic deserves a longer video.

Strategy

Three kinds of attention, three jobs.

The four workstreams look unrelated until you sort them by how the audience arrives. Someone scrolling, someone following a person and someone typing a question are in three different mindsets, and each needs a different format, tone and measure of success.

Why this matters for a comparison site

Social builds the audience. Search captures the demand.

A comparison site earns when someone compares and chooses. Search content meets people at that moment. Social and founder content do an earlier job: they make Finder a name people already recognise and trust when the search happens. Content planning is what stops the two from competing for the same slot.
