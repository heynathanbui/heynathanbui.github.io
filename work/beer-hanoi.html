01 · The challenge

A familiar name, made fresh.

Zee Agency ran a national TVC campaign for Beer Hanoi: a long-form commercial plus a set of shorter cutdowns for separate platform distribution.

Beer Hanoi is a heritage brand in one of Vietnam’s most crowded categories. The challenge was to make a familiar name feel fresh without losing what loyal drinkers love about it.

Beer brands in a crowded category tend to look and sound alike. Tết is the most competitive window of the year, when every brand is talking about family. To stand out, the ad needed something competitors couldn’t easily copy: a sound people recognise as Beer Hanoi.

Objective

Make Beer Hanoi feel like home: a familiar sound that brings people back to the family table.

02 · The story

“Dẫu ở đâu, con cũng sẽ về.”

“Wherever I am, I’ll come home.” From the campaign’s 0:45 film, Tết này con sẽ về.

The campaign is about family reunion: people coming home from far away to the people who matter. The sound had one job, to make that homecoming feel familiar before you even see the table.

Why sound

The strategic case for the ear.

01 · Memory

Familiar sounds are memory cues

A clink, laughter or a known voice is recognised almost instantly and carries emotional associations. Sound can trigger “home” before the visuals have made the point.

02 · Distinctiveness

Consistency builds a brand asset

Brand assets only work when they repeat. Keeping the same voice and sound world in every cut lets the brand be recognised even when the visuals change.

03 · Format

Cutdowns compress the story

A short cutdown can’t retell the whole homecoming. The sound has to carry the emotional shorthand of the long film, so the feeling survives the edit.

One sound, three films.

The campaign ran as a 4:46 hero film and two cutdowns across TV, YouTube and Facebook. Each cut tells a different slice of the homecoming, but the voice and the sound of the family table carry through all three, so every version is recognisably the same brand.

Each step down removes story, so sound does more of the work. In 30 seconds there is no time to build the journey home; the familiar voice and the table have to deliver the feeling immediately.

One idea, three entry points

The hero film builds the emotion, and the cutdowns repeat it at lengths that suit TV slots and social feeds. More chances to see the same brand cue means stronger memory.

The line is the brief

“Dẫu ở đâu con cũng sẽ về” puts the insight straight into the title: the reunion matters more than the journey, and Beer Hanoi sits at the table where it ends.

How I’d prove the sound worked

Recommendations, not results: the measurement I would add next time.

  1. Brand lift. Run a YouTube Brand Lift study to measure ad recall and brand awareness against a control group.
  2. Sound-on vs sound-off. Compare completion rates by placement to see where the sonic cues are actually heard, and add captions where they aren’t.
  3. Recognition test. Play the audio without visuals to a sample audience and measure how many correctly name Beer Hanoi.
  4. Retention by cut length. Compare audience retention for the long film and each cutdown to find where the story holds attention.
